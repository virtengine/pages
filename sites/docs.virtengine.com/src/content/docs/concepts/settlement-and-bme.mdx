---
title: Settlement, Credits & Issuance
description: Distinguish VE primary issuance, VCC service credits, escrow payouts, and net burning.
---

VE (`uve`) is VirtEngine's native asset. VirtEngine Compute Credit, VCC
(`uvcc`), names the service-accounting unit. VirtEngine does not depend on
Akash's compute token. Naming a credit does not establish a peg or redemption.

## Four different flows

| Flow | What changes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary issuance | New allocations under proposed human eligibility rules | Purchasing power or adequate resource supply. |
| VE/VCC conversion | Intended conversion between monetary and service units | A new human entitlement or permanent VE destruction. |
| Escrow settlement | Ownership of funds released under agreement rules | Correct computation or a supply reduction. |
| Net burning | Native units destroyed after all reissuance | Beneficial agents or universal economic dependence. |

Provider-facing policy calls for the **full agreed amount from escrow**, with
no platform deduction. Network transaction fees apply separately to chain
actions. Actual payout uses the agreement's supported denomination; VE and
service credits are not interchangeable units.

## What is implemented?

The checked BME conversion handlers return pending without executing bank/oracle
conversion. Billing transfers funds to providers and can burn a configurable
settlement fee, which defaults to zero. Generic mint/burn helpers and accounting
exist. These paths do not establish a permanent consumption sink for every
compute purchase. See [BME implementation evidence](/modules/bme/).

## Proposed human-led primary issuance

Each proposed 15-unit batch allocates 14 to eligible active verified humans
and 1 to Foundation stewardship. That share is part of the batch, not an
additional mint. Initial supply is zero and there is no fixed cap. Staking
does not independently authorize new primary issuance. See
[tokenomics](/concepts/tokenomics/) and [issuance-policy authority](/modules/issuancepolicy/).

## Why investigate the relationship?

Personhood-rooted primary allocations could provide claims on output when wage
competition becomes less reliable. Recurring resource consumption could
potentially connect machine activity to demand for those claims. Test net
native burning, outside compute, reserves, entitlement control, purchasing
capacity, inclusion and protection of the rules before claiming that effect.

Foundation public-benefit restrictions, stake-weighted chain governance and
proposed agent controls have different authority. Economic incentives alone
do not establish safety or broad benefit. Explore the
[interactive research conditions](https://det.io/learn/economic-invariants).
